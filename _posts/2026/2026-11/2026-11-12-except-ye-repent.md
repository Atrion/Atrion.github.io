The Gospel meetings at the Rosebank Gospel Hall here on Prince Edward Island have been running for a few weeks now, just wrapping up their third week, and my mind has drifted back to the time I was saved.

If you’re interested in the full story, I’ve written it out in two ways. There’s a short [tract, *My Journey to Christ*](https://gospel.fromgravelhill.ca/2026/11/11/tract-my-journey-to-christ/), and a [longer account on Heaven4Sure](https://heaven4sure.com/my-journey-to-christ-marc-macarthur/). Those tell about the loss that started my search. This post is about something smaller: the particular message I was listening to when the Lord opened my eyes.

<!--more-->

Now don’t get me wrong. No preacher, message, audio or video can ever save anyone. Only the Lord Jesus Christ can.

> **Acts 4:12 (KJV)**
>
> *“Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved.”*

But He used this message to finally get through to me, and I’d like to tell you about it.


On the morning of March 10, 2023, I was sitting in a car outside the QEH. I’d been going to Gospel meetings for a while by then, reading my Bible and asking questions. I understood more and more of what I was hearing, but I couldn’t make it my own. I felt like I was standing at a door and couldn’t work out how to walk through.

To take my mind off where I was, I put on a Gospel podcast. It was an *Anchor Point* radio broadcast with a preacher named Jack Hay, called *Repent and Believe*. I listened with my eyes closed.


<BR>

## Repent or perish

Mr. Hay took his text from Luke 13. Some people told the Lord Jesus about Galileans whom Pilate had killed while they were offering sacrifices, and the unspoken thought was that they must have been terrible sinners for that to happen. The Lord brought up another tragedy too, the eighteen who died when a tower fell in Siloam. His answer to both was the same:

> **Luke 13:2-3 (KJV)**
>
> *“Suppose ye that these Galileans were sinners above all the Galileans, because they suffered such things? I tell you, Nay: but, except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish.”*

It’s not a comfortable verse, and Mr. Hay didn’t soften it. But he explained something I had missed. Repentance isn’t just feeling sorry, and it isn’t trying harder. It’s a change of mind about myself and about God, and a willingness to turn from sin. I couldn’t do that on my own either. I’d already learned how many times I’d tried and failed.

> **John 8:34, 36 (KJV)**
>
> *“Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin. … If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”*

Then he joined repentance to faith, as Paul did, “repentance toward God, and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ” (Acts 20:21). Faith in a Saviour who didn’t stay in the tomb. Mr. Hay told of an old man in Northern Ireland who was asked how he got saved. He could barely speak, but he said, “The work was done, and I rested there.” That one stuck with me. I wasn’t being asked to do the work. I was being asked to rest in the One who had finished it.


<BR>

## “You wouldn’t”

Two other parts of the message came right to me.

The first was the fig tree in Luke 13. The owner came looking for fruit year after year and found none, yet the keeper asked for one more year. God had been patient with me. I’d had church as a boy, a little New Testament that sat unread on a shelf, a condolence card with John 3:16 in it, and a handful of Gospel meetings. Every one of those was another opportunity.

The second was the way the chapter ends, with the Lord looking over Jerusalem: “How often would I have gathered thy children together, as a hen doth gather her brood under her wings, and ye would not!” (Luke 13:34). He wasn’t unwilling to save them. They were unwilling to come.

Mr. Hay also described four pictures on a Gospel invitation card: *too happy, too busy, too tired, too late*. I didn’t want to be the one who put it off until the last of those. He also reminded us that the Lord is “not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance” (2 Peter 3:9).


<BR>

## What I did

I hit pause, right there in the car, and began to speak out loud. I told God I was a sinner and that I needed help. I told Him I believed Christ suffered and died for me and that through Him my sins could be forgiven. It was the verse in Joel that undid me:

> **Joel 2:32 (KJV)**
>
> *“And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the LORD shall be delivered.”*

I felt a real weight lift off my chest, and I knew that something significant had happened.

I wasn’t saved because of Mr. Hay’s preaching, or because of the words I said in a parked car. I was saved because the Lord Jesus Christ died for my sins and rose again, and because I finally stopped keeping Him at arm’s length and trusted Him. He did the work. I’m the one who rested there.


<BR>

## And now, Rosebank

That’s why the meetings at Rosebank mean so much to me this fall. The [Fall 2026 Gospel Series](https://olearygospelhall.ca/2026/10/25/rosebank-gospel-hall-fall-2026-gospel-series/) has run for 3 weeks, with Brody Thibodeau and David Hierlihy, and others joining them. Behind every message is a person like I was: someone who has heard it, maybe agrees with it, and has never turned and trusted Christ for themselves.

If that’s you, I’d ask you gently not to wait. I don’t know what has brought you to this page, but I know what brought me to that door. You don’t need to understand every part of it first. Call on the Lord and He will save you.

> **Acts 2:21 (KJV)**
>
> *“And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved.”*

If you’re on the Island, I’d encourage you to come out to [Rosebank Gospel Hall](https://rosebankgospelhall.ca/) and hear the Gospel for yourself. If you can’t make it, the [audio from each night](https://olearygospelhall.ca/2026/10/25/rosebank-gospel-hall-fall-2026-gospel-series/) is on the series page. You can also [listen to the message that opened my eyes](https://spoti.fi/4anGTaP).
