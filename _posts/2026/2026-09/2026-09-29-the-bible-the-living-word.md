> **Hebrews 4:12 KJV**
>
> *“For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword.”*

The Bible is an extraordinary book. It is made up of sixty-six individual books, written by about forty human writers across roughly fifteen centuries. Those writers came from very different backgrounds and lived in very different circumstances. Among them were kings, shepherds, prophets, fishermen, a physician, a tax collector, and others whose names are not known with certainty. They lived under different governments, in different lands, and in different generations, yet when their writings are brought together, they do not read like a collection of unrelated religious documents.

They tell one unfolding story.

<!--more-->

The Bible begins with creation and ends with a new creation. It begins with God and man together and ends with God dwelling with redeemed men and women forever. Between those two points is the great story of sin, judgment, promise, sacrifice, grace, redemption, and the Person and work of the Son of God, the Lord Jesus Christ.

Many men held the pen, but one God gave the Word.

<BR>

## One Book, One Divine Author

Scripture came to us over many generations. Moses knew the wilderness. David knew both the shepherd’s field and the throne. Daniel lived amid Babylonian and Persian power. Peter was a fisherman from Galilee, Luke was a physician, Paul wrote some of his epistles from prison, and John received the Revelation while exiled on Patmos.

These men were not gathered in one place planning a single manuscript. Many lived centuries apart and never knew one another, yet their writings fit together with remarkable unity.

This does not mean that every writer sounds the same. Scripture preserves their individuality. John does not write like Paul, and David does not write like Moses. Their vocabulary, experiences, and backgrounds remain, but God used them to give exactly what He intended.

Peter explains it this way:

> **2 Peter 1:21 KJV**
>
> *“For the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man: but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost.”*

Paul writes:

> **2 Timothy 3:16 KJV**
>
> *“All scripture is given by inspiration of God.”*

The Bible is not merely inspiring. It is inspired. Its authority does not rest on the intelligence of its writers, the approval of a church, or the judgment of scholars. Its authority comes from its source.

God has spoken.

<BR>

## One Continuous Story

The unity of Scripture becomes even clearer when we follow its story from beginning to end.

Genesis opens with the words:

> **Genesis 1:1 KJV**
>
> *“In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.”*

By the third chapter, sin has entered the world. Fellowship with God has been broken, judgment has come, and death has entered. Yet even there, God begins to reveal His purpose of redemption. A promised Seed will come.

As the Old Testament continues, that promise becomes clearer. Abraham is promised blessing through his seed. Judah is identified. David receives promises concerning a coming King. The prophets speak of One who will suffer, bear sin, and reign.

Then Matthew opens with these words:

> **Matthew 1:1 KJV**
>
> *“The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham.”*

The New Testament is not beginning a different story. It is declaring that the promised One has come.

<BR>

## Christ at the Centre

The Bible speaks about many things, but its great centre is Christ. The Lord Jesus Himself said:

> **John 5:39 KJV**
>
> *“Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me.”*

The Old Testament prepares the way for Him. We see Him in promise and prophecy, in sacrifice and in picture. The tabernacle, the offerings, the priesthood, and the history of Israel all contribute to the unfolding revelation that reaches its fullness in Christ.

The Gospels present Him. The Epistles explain the meaning of His finished work. Revelation shows us His final triumph.

One of the clearest threads running through Scripture is the picture of the lamb. In Genesis 22, Isaac asks Abraham, *“Where is the lamb for a burnt offering?”* Abraham answers that God will provide Himself a lamb. In Exodus 12, the blood of the Passover lamb shelters Israel from judgment. Isaiah speaks of One who would be *“brought as a lamb to the slaughter.”*

Then John the Baptist sees the Lord Jesus and declares:

> **John 1:29 KJV**
>
> *“Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”*

Peter later speaks of:

> **1 Peter 1:19 KJV**
>
> *“the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot.”*

Finally, Revelation presents *“a Lamb as it had been slain.”*

Genesis asks, *“Where is the lamb?”* The Gospels answer, *“Behold the Lamb.”* Revelation shows us the Lamb upon the throne.

That is not a collection of disconnected images. It is one unfolding revelation.

<BR>

## The Living Word

Hebrews describes the Word of God in striking terms:

> **Hebrews 4:12 KJV**
>
> *“For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword.”*

“Quick” in the language of the King James Bible means living. The Bible is ancient, but it is not simply an ancient work of literature. Its words were written centuries ago, yet they continue to address the heart and conscience with a relevance undiminished by their age.

This does not mean that Scripture changes its meaning from one generation to another. The truth God gave remains the same. What changes is the reader. A familiar passage may take on renewed weight as circumstances change, understanding grows, or the Spirit of God brings its truth more clearly before us.

Most books are examined by their readers. Scripture has a remarkable way of examining the reader. Hebrews says that the Word of God is:

> **Hebrews 4:12 KJV**
>
> *“a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart.”*

The Bible does more than communicate information. It exposes motives, corrects thinking, challenges assumptions, and brings the conscience before God. A familiar passage may reveal something we had overlooked or correct an attitude that had quietly become settled.

Scripture also gives direction. David wrote:

> **Psalm 119:105 KJV**
>
> *“Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.”*

The Bible does not give a specific verse for every circumstance we will ever face, but it gives the truth and principles by which those circumstances can be judged. The more thoroughly Scripture shapes our thinking, the less dependent we become upon whatever happens to be popular, persuasive, or accepted at the moment.

We therefore do not read the Bible merely to discover what people believed long ago. We come to it knowing that the God who gave the Word still uses that same Word today.

<BR>

## A Word to Be Handled Carefully

Because Scripture is the Word of God, it deserves careful treatment. Paul told Timothy:

> **2 Timothy 2:15 KJV**
>
> *“Study to shew thyself approved unto God... rightly dividing the word of truth.”*

A verse may be quoted accurately and still be used wrongly if removed from its context. An idea can easily be read into a passage rather than drawn from it, and a tradition can become so familiar that we assume Scripture says something it never actually says.

Careful Bible study therefore asks what a passage says, who is speaking, to whom it was written, what surrounds it, and how it fits with the rest of Scripture. Difficult portions should be considered alongside clearer ones, and doctrine should not be built upon an isolated phrase while ignoring the larger teaching of the Word.

This is especially important in an assembly. No preacher, Bible teacher, elder, commentary, conference address, or respected name carries the authority of Scripture itself. The believers at Berea were commended because they:

> **Acts 17:11 KJV**
>
> *“searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.”*

Even when Paul was speaking, the Scriptures remained the test. That remains a sound pattern for us.

<BR>

## A Word That Endures

The world surrounding the Bible has changed almost beyond recognition since its human writers first put words to parchment. Kingdoms have risen and disappeared. Languages have changed. Philosophies have come and gone. Scripture has moved from handwritten manuscripts to printed books, and now to computers and phones carried in our pockets.

The medium has changed, but the Word has not. Isaiah wrote:

> **Isaiah 40:8 KJV**
>
> *“The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever.”*

The endurance of Scripture is more than the remarkable survival of an ancient text. The Word endures because the God who gave it does not change. Human knowledge develops, theories are revised, and opinions continually shift, but the Word of God does not wait for a later generation to improve upon what God meant to say.

That is why the Bible should occupy more than a ceremonial place in the Christian life. It is to be read carefully, studied patiently, believed, and allowed to shape our thinking. A lifetime can be spent reading it without exhausting its depth, not because its meaning continually changes, but because we continue to discover how closely its truths are connected.

<BR>

## The Living Word of God

The Bible is remarkable in its composition: sixty-six books, about forty human writers, and roughly fifteen centuries. Its unity is remarkable. Its preservation is remarkable. From Genesis to Revelation its themes unfold into one continuous story centred upon the Lord Jesus Christ.

But its greatest claim is not simply that it is a remarkable book.

It is the Word of God.

The same Scriptures that spoke to generations long before us continue to teach, correct, guide, search, and strengthen today. The Lord Jesus said:

> **Matthew 24:35 KJV**
>
> *“Heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away.”*

Many human writers, but one Divine Author. Written across centuries, yet telling one unfolding story. Ancient in its origin, yet living in its voice.

The Bible is not simply a book that has survived.

It is **the living Word of the living God**.
